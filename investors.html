Kaldrix and Zentrix: signed limits for AI agents that move money.

A person signs what an agent may spend. The limit holds even if the agent is fooled, because no AI model decides it. A second system, Zentrix, checks it again and moves the money. Built first for bank transfers in Nigeria.

Why now, and who else is here

Agents are starting to pay. The rails are being standardised.

Open protocols for agent payments are appearing (x402 for paying over the web, AP2 for signed payment mandates, MCP for giving agents tools). They describe how an agent asks and pays. They leave the question of who allowed it, how much, and who can stop it to the layer on top. That is the layer we build.

Login and multi-factor tools

Prove who is asking. They stop at the door: a logged-in agent can usually do whatever the account allows.

Agent wallets with rules

Hold the money inside their system under their rules, mostly in dollars, stablecoins and cards.

Monitors and approval gateways

Watch the agent or use a model to judge each request. A model can be fooled; a signed number cannot.

Other teams are in this space and some are well funded. We do not claim to be the only one or the first. The doing-nothing option, a person checking every payment, is the real competitor. Our edge is hard signed limits that hold when the agent is fooled, exact counting, a second check before money moves, and a design for bank transfers where local providers matter.

Risks, said plainly

What could go wrong.

  • A very small team. Speed is high, and so is dependence on a few people. Hires are planned after the first paid pilot.
  • Security. A security product that has not been independently reviewed is not trusted yet. The review is the first priority after the first partners.
  • Licences. Moving real money in Nigeria and elsewhere needs a licensed provider or our own licence. The plan is to work through licensed providers first.
  • Bigger players. Card networks, payment companies and well-funded startups could add the same idea. Our protection is exactness, speed of learning from design partners, and local focus.
  • Demand not proven. We have conversations, not commitments. The design-partner programme exists to prove or disprove demand quickly.

Questions investors ask first

Is there any revenue or are there customers yet?

No. There are no customers and no revenue yet. What exists is a working test service with a pretend bank, public proof you can check, and a plan to turn the first design partners into paid pilots.

Has it had an independent security review?

No. That is the first thing a serious partner will ask for and it is on the plan. Everything the founder tested himself, including attacks written to win, is published. It is not a substitute for an outside review.

Is real money involved?

Not yet. The payment provider in the demos is a simulation, and a real provider's test mode was used once. Real money comes only after a security review and a signed agreement with a live provider.

How is this different from agent wallets or login tools?

Login tools say who is asking. Agent wallets hold money under rules. Kaldrix puts a signed limit on the payment itself, decided without an AI model, and Zentrix checks it again before the money moves.
