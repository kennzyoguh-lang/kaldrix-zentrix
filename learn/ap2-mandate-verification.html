AP2 mandate verification for developers

AP2, Google's Agent Payments Protocol, records what a person allowed an agent to pay as signed mandates. Two jobs follow: verify the signatures, then check the actual payment against the constraints inside them.

The idea in plain words

AP2 describes a person's permission as a chain of signed tokens. In the version 0.2 documents there is an open payment mandate, which holds the limits, and a closed payment mandate, which describes one actual payment. A verifier has two jobs:

  1. Verify the chain. The signatures are valid, the keys are trusted, and the closed mandate really descends from the open one. A library that implements the delegate SD-JWT draft does this; Google's own Python reference SDK does it.
  2. Check the constraints. Does the closed payment satisfy what the open mandate allows? That is the step the specification calls for after the signatures are good, and it is where mistakes cost money.

Constraints worth checking

ConstraintWhat it limits
payment.amount_rangeMinimum and maximum for one payment.
payment.allowed_payeesWho may be paid.
payment.allowed_payment_instruments and allowed_pispsWhich instruments and payment initiation providers may be used.
payment.budgetA total across payments.
payment.agent_recurrenceRepeating payments, which needs an amount range and a budget.
payment.reference and payment.execution_dateWhat the payment is for, and when it may run.

Rules that are easy to get wrong

  • An unknown constraint must fail. If a verifier ignores a constraint it does not understand, a mandate can say "no more than X" and the verifier lets it through. The specification requires failing.
  • Units differ. A budget may be in major units while payment amounts are in minor units. Mixing them gives a limit that is a hundred times too loose or too tight.
  • Never loosen a limit while translating. If you turn a mandate into a local policy and some constraint cannot be expressed, list it as not enforced, and refuse a mandate that leaves out the amount limit instead of giving it an unlimited policy.

What we built

Our public x402 guard repository includes a constraint checker for AP2 version 0.2. It does not verify signatures (use a library that implements the draft for that). It checks the closed payment against the open mandate's constraints, fails on unknown constraints, and can convert an open mandate's limits into a policy for the x402 guard without ever loosening them. It was written from the published documents and the reference SDK's source and tested to follow them. It was not run against the reference SDK's own test suite, and it is not endorsed by Google, the FIDO Alliance or anyone else. AP2 version 0.2 is described as pre-release and may change.

Why a signed-limit layer still matters

A mandate says what a person allowed. Something still has to count the spending as it happens, ask a human for the large ones, stop on revocation, and re-check at the moment of payment. That is the layer Kaldrix and Zentrix build, and it can sit beside AP2 and x402 rather than replace them. See how payment authorization works.

Questions people ask

What does an AP2 verifier have to do?

Verify the signature chain, then check that the actual payment satisfies the constraints in the open mandate. Unknown constraints must fail.

What is the difference between an open and a closed AP2 mandate?

In version 0.2 the open mandate holds the limits a person set; the closed mandate describes one actual payment made under them.

Does the Kaldrix checker verify signatures?

No. It checks the constraints after a library that implements the delegate SD-JWT draft has verified the chain.

Is AP2 final?

Version 0.2 is described as pre-release and may change, so check the official specification before relying on any detail.
