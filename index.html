The problem

Agents can now spend money. Nobody can yet prove they should have.

Protocols exist for agents to message each other and to move funds. What is missing is proof: that the agent stayed inside its instructions, and that a person (or a company) really gave it that authority. Without proof, banks and card issuers freeze agent payments, and companies cannot hand agents real budgets.

Agents get tricked

A hostile email, a poisoned web page or a bad tool result can steer an agent into paying the wrong person.

Banks can not tell who approved

When an agent drains funds, the bank sees a command and no proof of who allowed it. Fraud and chargeback risk lands on the bank, so it says no.

Money rails do not match

An agent holding stablecoins can not pay a regional vendor that only takes local bank transfers.

The two products

One signs the authority. One clears the money.

Kaldrix authorization

A signed, limited permission for an agent.

  • Who may be paid, how much per payment and in all, for what purpose, until when.
  • Handed on only in a smaller form, never a bigger one.
  • Big payments wait for a named person. Cancel at any moment.
  • Every decision goes on a tamper-evident record.

Zentrix conditional escrow and execution clearinghouse

Money moves only when the agent proves it fulfilled its exact instructions.

  • Sits between autonomous agents and real-world financial rails.
  • Turns execution logic into verifiable, settled transactions.
  • Never pays twice, and says "unknown" instead of guessing.
  • Fails over to another provider only when it is certain nothing was sent.

Zentrix in three parts

Escrow, proof and settlement, for agents.

Each part says plainly how far along it is.

1

Conditional escrow

Basic payment triggers are fine for tiny purchases, like fractions of a cent for an API call. Real business is less predictable: an agent that orders inventory or runs a multi-step purchase needs funds locked, and released only when the task is verified to have stayed inside its exact scope, with no hijacking or prompt manipulation.

Working today A payment is released only after its signed proof checks pass, and payments above your limit wait for a person.
Designed next A locked escrow vault for multi-step tasks.
2

Proof-of-intent compliance envelope

Banks and card issuers freeze autonomous transactions because nobody can verify who approved the command. Every Zentrix payment carries a signed envelope created at the point of execution: the parent authority, the scope, and the time limits the agent was given before the money moved.

Working in the test service Each successful payment returns a signed envelope. You can open one in the live demo.
3

Cross-rail settlement

An agent holding stablecoins on an international cloud can not buy from a vendor that only accepts a local bank transfer. Zentrix takes the settlement request, handles conversion, and settles onto local networks such as Nigeria's NIP, Kenya's M-Pesa or Brazil's PIX.

Working today Nigerian bank transfers through Paystack in real test mode.
Built, live test pending Flutterwave.
Planned M-Pesa, PIX and stablecoin conversion.

How it works

Four steps, and the agent can not skip any.

1

A person signs a limit

"This supplier, this much, until this time." That is the permission.

2

The agent asks to pay

Each payment needs its own proof, tied to the exact amount and supplier.

3

Two checks, then a record

Kaldrix checks the permission. Zentrix checks again, then pays and returns a signed proof envelope.

4

A person stays in charge

Above an amount you choose, the payment waits for your approval. Cancel any time.

Works with

Built to sit on top of the protocols, not to fight them.

These are the integration paths we are designing. No partnership or integration with any company below is claimed today.

x402 (Coinbase)

x402 moves funds whenever a server returns a payment request. It does not check whether the agent was tricked into paying the wrong address. Zentrix would sit in the middle as a facilitator, verifying the execution proof before the transaction is broadcast.

Planned

AP2 (Google)

AP2 signs the user's mandate before an agent buys. Settlement still depends on the rails underneath. Zentrix would turn a mandate into a strictly bounded payout on bank networks, where card rails do not reach.

Planned

Agent card platforms

Virtual cards with daily caps can not stop an agent from spending its allowance on a bad target. Zentrix would act as an authorization sidecar, checking that the transaction matches what the agent is actually running before the card authorization clears.

Why it is different

The limit is outside the agent, and the proof is tied to the payment.

Many agent-security tools watch or filter what an agent does. Kaldrix and Zentrix take a different route.

The proof is tied to the payment

Each payment needs a signature over the exact amount, supplier and currency. A proof made for ₦200 can not be stretched to pay ₦450.

The agent can not grant itself anything

Its key can pay and read payments, nothing else. It can not add suppliers, widen a permission or approve its own payment. The service enforces that.

People stay in the loop where it matters

Above an amount you choose, a named person (or two) must approve that exact payment.

Built for places where banks are unreliable

Nigeria first. Zentrix will not pay twice when a bank times out, and it only tries another provider when it is certain nothing was sent.
